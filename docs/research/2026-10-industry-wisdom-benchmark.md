# Industry wisdom benchmark for OpenDAP boards

- **Date:** 2026-10-24
- **Status:** Desk research, asked by the maintainers on 2026-10-24. Nothing here is a decision. It collects what engineers with long practice in board design publish about the knowledge that simulation and DRC do not give, and checks each practice against the T1 documents of that date. The T1 gaps and proposed constraint values are in the board repository: [industry wisdom: T1 gaps](https://github.com/opendap-project/board/blob/main/docs/research/2026-10-industry-wisdom-t1-gaps.md). The reviews run from the [design review checklist](../specs/design-review-checklist.md).
- **Scope:** every OpenDAP board. The application column uses the T1 main board as of the [electronics plan](https://github.com/opendap-project/t1/blob/main/docs/plans/2026-10-electronics-plan.md), [PCB design constraints v0.4](https://github.com/opendap-project/board/blob/main/docs/specs/pcb-design-constraints.md), the [electronics integration and verification spec](https://github.com/opendap-project/t1/blob/main/docs/specs/electronics-integration-verification.md), BOM v1.7 and board ADRs 0010, 0012, 0014, 0015 and 0016.
- **Method:** primary sources first (books, manufacturer application notes, standards, the authors' own articles and talks). Web sources were retrieved on 2026-10-24 and are marked *retrieved*. Books were not retrieved: their practices are cited by book and chapter topic, and any chapter number, page or figure from them is **UNVERIFIED** until the text is checked. Secondary sources (summaries, press, forum threads) are marked *secondary*. Arithmetic is shown as such.

## 0. Summary

1. **The experts warn against rule lists.** Hubing: "a board that complies with 90% of the guidelines may be much worse than a board that complies with only one or two guidelines" [HUB1]. Bogatin's first two rules: the answer is usually "it depends", and myth is separated from reality by "putting in the numbers" [BOG20]. Each practice below therefore carries its mechanism, so a reviewer can tell when it applies. Three common rules are named as myths by Hubing (20-H, 45° corners, guard traces) [HUB2]; one T1 constraint leans on a guard trace (PCB-I-09).
2. **Return current is the single idea behind most layout practice.** Stack-up, grounding, ESD, EMC and audio crosstalk all reduce to: know where every current returns, and keep that path short, continuous and away from sensitive references (Ott [OTT] ch. on grounding and on PCB stack-up; Hartley [HART]; Putzeys [PUTZ]; TI [SPRACP4A]). The T1 documents state the principle (PCB-A-01, PCB-S-04) but have no artefact that maps the return paths of the large currents (headphone loads, amplifier rails, charger, HAT, USB to a computer).
3. **Audio: the class-AB rail current is the classic hidden distortion source.** Self's "rail decoupling" and "induction" distortions [SELF-APA] apply to the six OPA1622: their rail currents are rectified copies of the output, and where those currents return and run decides the distortion floor. The T1 constraints cover the DAC side from the CS43198 datasheet, not this.
4. **Power: what the converter does at light load matters as much as the hot loop.** The LMR33630C has no forced-PWM mode [TI-E2E-LMR], so its pulse-skipping bursts at idle may land in the audio band (BOM line 116 already marks this **UNVERIFIED**). A board-wide frequency plan and a light-load strategy are missing.
5. **Bring-up and test access are design decisions, not afterthoughts.** Ganssle [GANS] and bunnie Huang [BUNNIE-EX] both argue that debug and test access cost little when designed in and are very expensive after. The T1 has test pads per rail and UART/SWD pads; it lacks rail isolation links for first power-up, reset/boot test points and scope ground points.
6. **Battery safety beyond the pack:** connector mating order, insertion transients on ceramic capacitors [AN88], fuse breaking capacity, and single-fault analysis of the charge path (IEC 62368-1 Annex M, *secondary* [62368-SEC]) are not yet in the T1 documents. The pack-level criteria (ADR 0014) are.

## 1. How to read the tables

Each practice has an ID `IW-<domain>-<nn>`, the rule, why it works (mechanism), the source, its application to the T1, and the T1 status:

- **Covered:** a T1 constraint, ADR, BOM line or verification row already does it (named).
- **Partial:** the T1 does part of it, or states it without a value, check or owner.
- **Missing:** nothing in the T1 documents does it.
- **Contradicts:** a T1 document says something the source argues against.
- **N/A:** does not apply to the T1, with the reason.

Gaps (Partial, Missing, Contradicts) are carried to the board research note with a proposed constraint ID or plan task and an RFC 0010 quality level.

## 2. Stack-up and return paths

| ID | Practice | Why | Source | T1 application | T1 status |

| IW-SR-01 | Every signal layer sits one dielectric away from a solid ground plane; power planes sit tightly against ground | Above roughly 100 kHz the return current flows in the plane directly under the trace, confined to the dielectric between them; a reference two or three dielectrics away lets the field spread and couple | Hartley, AltiumLive talk "How to achieve proper grounding" and stack-up podcast [HART]; Ott, chapter on PCB layout and stack-up [OTT] | PCB-S-04 asks this for high-speed and analog-audio layers only. | IW-SR-02 | No high-speed or sensitive route over a split or void in its reference plane; if unavoidable, stitching capacitors (≤ 1 µF) across the split next to the crossing | The return current must detour around the void: larger loop, impedance step, radiation | TI SPRACP4A §2.4 [SPRACP4A], *retrieved*; Rockchip HSPCB p. 8, transcribed in the [RK3566 layout rules](https://github.com/opendap-project/board/blob/main/docs/research/2026-10-rk3566-layout-rules.md) | PCB-A-01 (one ground, no splits) and PCB-I-09 (MCLK, no reference change) state it; no check enforces it on the layout | Partial |

## 3. Grounding and partitioning for audio

| ID | Practice | Why | Source | T1 application | T1 status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| IW-GA-01 | One ground plane; partition by placement, never by splitting | A split forces return currents of signals crossing it into large loops; partitioning keeps digital return currents out of the analog area naturally | Ott, chapter on mixed-signal layout [OTT]; ADI MT-031 [MT031], *retrieved* | PCB-A-01 | Covered |
| IW-GA-02 | A converter's AGND and DGND pins go together to the analog ground plane at the chip | The pin names describe the inside of the chip; separating them outside injects digital current noise into the analog reference | ADI MT-031 [MT031] | PCB-A-01, PCB-A-02 (CS43198 common ground area and pad vias) | Covered |
| IW-GA-03 | "Ground" is not a sink: draw every current loop, and route each return deliberately | Noise is a current flowing through a shared impedance; naming a net GND does not tell where its current flows | Putzeys, "The G-word, or how to get your audio off the ground", *Linear Audio* vol. 5 [PUTZ] (title and volume *retrieved*, text not); Hartley [HART] (*secondary* summary) | The T1 has the rule (PCB-A-01) but no return-current map of its large currents: headphone loads, amplifier rails, charger and battery, HAT 5 V, USB to a computer | Missing |
| IW-GA-04 | Return the class-AB output stage's rail decoupling capacitors to the power ground point, not to the input or feedback reference | Each rail of a class-AB output stage draws a rectified copy of the output current; its decoupling capacitor's ground return carries that distorted current, and sharing it with the signal ground adds distortion ("Distortion 5, rail decoupling") | Self, *Audio Power Amplifier Design*, the "eight distortions" [SELF-APA]; summary of distortions 5–7 [SELF-8D], *secondary*, *retrieved* | The OPA1622 has a class-AB output; ADR 0015's simulation shows the rail current is a rectified sine with a 433 mA peak. No constraint covers where the six packages' rail capacitors return relative to the gain-network reference (BOM line 37/108) | Missing |
| IW-GA-05 | Keep rail and output current paths away from, and not parallel to, input and feedback traces | The rectified rail currents couple inductively into input or feedback loops ("Distortion 6, induction") | Self [SELF-APA]; [SELF-8D] *secondary* | PCB-A-09 asks symmetry of the paralleled halves, not separation of rail current paths from the difference-amplifier inputs | Missing |
| IW-GA-06 | In a single-ended output with a shared return (3.5 mm TRS), the common return impedance sets the channel crosstalk | The return of one channel's load current develops a voltage across the shared impedance that the other channel sees: crosstalk ≈ R_common / Z_load. With 16 Ω: 100 mΩ gives −44 dB, 10 mΩ −64 dB, 5 mΩ −70 dB (arithmetic: 20·log10(R/Z)) | Self, *Small Signal Audio Design*, headphone amplifier chapter [SELF-SSAD] (**UNVERIFIED** chapter content); arithmetic | PCB-A-03 senses the CS43198 REFA/REFB at the jack ground pin. The OPA1622 difference amplifiers sit between the DAC and the jack (BOM line 19, 37): whether their reference resistors also return to the jack ground, or to local ground, decides whether the board's common return is sensed out or adds crosstalk. The documents do not say | Partial |
| IW-GA-07 | An external ground connection (USB to a computer, a line output to an amplifier) closes a ground loop through the device; plan where that current runs | Noise current from the other equipment flows through the device's ground between the two connectors; if it crosses the audio reference it appears in the output | Putzeys [PUTZ]; Ott, grounding chapter [OTT] | USB-C and both jacks are on the same bottom edge (PCB-M-05). V4-06 measures the USB DAC mode ground loop; no layout rule keeps the USB-C ↔ jack ground current away from the DAC and amplifier reference | Partial |
| IW-GA-08 | Filter RF at the inputs of audio amplifiers and at cables that leave the device; choose op-amps with published EMI rejection | Op-amp input junctions rectify RF into a DC or audio-band shift (EMIRR); the input pins are the most sensitive | TI SBOA128 [SBOA128], *retrieved* | The headphone cable is an antenna about 1 m long next to a 2.4/5 GHz radio; nothing in the constraints provides RF filtering footprints at the jacks or measures EMIRR of the OPA1622 | Missing |

| ID | Practice | Why | Source | T1 application | T1 status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| IW-PI-01 | A decoupling capacitor's value matters less than its mounting loop: minimise loop area (pad, via, distance to the plane pair); place it on the side where the loop is smaller | Inductance is proportional to loop area; above a few MHz the capacitor's own value does not matter | Archambeault [ARCH], *retrieved*; Bogatin, *Signal and Power Integrity — Simplified* [BOG] (**UNVERIFIED** chapter) | PCB-P-10: SoC and LPDDR4X decoupling on the front under the ball field (ADR 0016), so every loop crosses the 1.0–1.2 mm board. | IW-PI-02 | Size the network from a target impedance (Z = ΔV / ΔI) over frequency, not from "one 100 nF per pin" | The supply tolerance and the load step set the impedance needed; folklore values over- or under-decouple | Bogatin [BOG]; ADI MT-101 [MT101], *retrieved* | For the SoC the T1 uses Rockchip's HDG values (PCB-P-10), the vendor's own analysis | Covered (by the vendor) |

## 6. Clocks and jitter

| ID | Practice | Why | Source | T1 application | T1 status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| IW-CK-01 | Bound the jitter a converter clock may carry from the signal frequency and the SNR target: SNR = −20·log10(2π·f·tj) | Timing error times the signal's slope is an amplitude error. At 20 kHz: 1 ns rms → 78 dB, 100 ps → 98 dB, 10 ps → 118 dB (arithmetic) | ADI MT-007 [MT007], *retrieved*; Dunn, "Jitter theory", Audio Precision Technote 23 [DUNN] (*secondary* reference) | ADR 0010 picks the lowest-phase-noise stocked oscillator. The CS43198's own jitter sensitivity is not published (**UNVERIFIED**), so the formula is an upper-bound check, not a pass criterion | Covered (choice) |
| IW-CK-02 | Keep the clock edge fast at the receiver: added jitter ≈ noise voltage / slew rate at the threshold | A slower edge converts the same supply or ground noise into more time error | Johnson and Graham, *High-Speed Digital Design* [JOH] (**UNVERIFIED** chapter); ADI MT-008 [MT008] | ADR 0010's passive attenuator lowers the amplitude and so the slew rate into XTI. E1.6 checks that the edge "does not slow down materially": no number | Partial |
| IW-CK-03 | Put the clock source next to the converter, make the converter the clock master, route the clock short, on an inner layer between ground planes, with no reference change | Every centimetre and every reference change adds coupling and noise-to-jitter conversion | Dunn [DUNN]; Ott [OTT] | ADR 0010 (DAC as master), PCB-I-09 | Covered |
| IW-CK-04 | Verify jitter at the analog output (J-test), not only at the clock pin | The DAC's response to jitter is what is heard; a clock-pin measurement does not show the DAC's rejection | Dunn [DUNN] (J-test is Dunn's) | ADR 0010 verification: J-test and THD+N captures | Covered |

## 10. Battery and power safety

| ID | Practice | Why | Source | T1 application | T1 status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| IW-BA-01 | Two independent layers limit the charge voltage, current and temperature, so a single fault (including software) cannot overcharge | IEC 62368-1 Annex M checks charging voltage, current and cell temperature under normal, abnormal and single-fault conditions | IEC 62368-1 Annex M, *secondary* summary [62368-SEC], *retrieved* (standard text **UNVERIFIED**); IEC 62133-2 | ADR 0014: charger regulation and TS window, pack PCM, board fuse; watchdog disabled, but the pack PCM covers a software fault. | IW-BA-02 | Leave the cell mechanical margin: no pressure on corners, no protrusion into the pocket, room for swelling | Samsung's Note 7 investigation found, in one battery, electrode deflection in a corner with "abnormal squeeze in battery corners" and thin separators; in the other, weld burrs piercing the separator | Samsung, 23 January 2017 findings with UL, Exponent and TÜV Rheinland, as reported [NOTE7], *secondary*, *retrieved* | ADR 0016 puts a metal plate between the board and the pocket; pocket and swelling allowance belong to the case | Partial (case input) |

## 11. Thermal practice without simulation

| ID | Practice | Why | Source | T1 application | T1 status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| IW-TH-01 | Bound the sustained power of a sealed handheld by its surface area before any simulation | Natural convection in air is 2–25 W/m²·K (Incropera, table of typical coefficients [INCROPERA], **UNVERIFIED** table number); radiation adds about 5.5 W/m²·K (arithmetic: 4·ε·σ·T³, ε = 0.9, T = 300 K). Take h ≈ 10 W/m²·K. The T1 body surface is 0.0189 m² (arithmetic: 2·(59.5·94.3 + 59.5·25 + 94.3·25) mm²), so about 0.19 W/K: 3 W sustained gives about 16 K mean surface rise (est.) | Incropera [INCROPERA]; arithmetic | ADR 0014's worst case includes "the system at 3 W"; the LMR33630C alone may lose 0.4–0.9 W (ADR 0015). | IW-TH-02 | Estimate junction temperature from a measured package-top temperature with ψJT; use θJA only to compare packages | θJA depends on the JEDEC test board's copper; ψJT is nearly independent of the board | TI SPRA953 [SPRA953], *retrieved* | BOM line 91 uses θJA (EVM 73.5 °C/W, JEDEC 97.2 °C/W) as an estimate, which is fine as an estimate; the bring-up method should use ψJT | Partial |
